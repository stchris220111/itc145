Bluetooth originated in 1994 at Ericsson, a Swedish telecommunications company. Engineers led by Jaap Haartsen sought to create a short-range wireless alternative to cables for connecting electronic devices. In 1998, the Bluetooth Special Interest Group (SIG) was formed by companies including Ericsson, IBM, Intel, Nokia, and Toshiba to develop and promote standards. The name "Bluetooth" comes from the 10th-century Danish king Harald Bluetooth, who united various Scandinavian tribes. The technology's creators chose the name because Bluetooth was intended to unite different communication devices and standards. Bluetooth 4.0, released in 2010, introduced Bluetooth Low Energy, while Bluetooth 5.x later increased range, speed, and broadcast capabilities. Bluetooth 6.0, introduced in 2024, added new capabilities including Channel Sounding, which can improve distance and positioning measurements.
